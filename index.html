Clinical trialSep 2026

SOFT Preop trial results presented at ASTRO 2026

William A. Hall, MD, presented the SOFT Preop randomized phase II trial as a late-breaking abstract at the 2026 ASTRO Annual Meeting. The trial compared five treatments of stereotactic body radiation therapy (SBRT) with 28 treatments of chemoradiation before surgery in 102 patients with pancreatic cancer. The proportion of patients with cancer in the lymph nodes at surgery was similar (41% with SBRT, 38% with chemoradiation), and patients who received SBRT reported better physical function and less fatigue. Median overall survival was 26 months with SBRT and 43 months with chemoradiation.

Our publicationAug 27, 2026

Resistance to daraxonrasib depends on the KRAS mutation

In Cancer Research, the McFall laboratory and collaborators, including our lab, showed that pancreatic cancers escape daraxonrasib by different routes. KRAS G12D tumors kept signaling through mutant KRAS and lost cyclophilin A, the protein the drug needs to act. KRAS G12R tumors switched to EGFR and wild-type RAS signaling, which made them sensitive to the MEK inhibitor trametinib. Our slice cultures of patient biopsies showed the same allele-specific response in human tissue, and a patient with KRAS G12R cancer achieved disease control on trametinib-based therapy after progressing on daraxonrasib.

Field newsAug 26, 2026

FDA approves daraxonrasib, the first RAS inhibitor for pancreatic cancer

The FDA approved daraxonrasib (Rasonque) for adults with metastatic pancreatic adenocarcinoma who have received prior systemic therapy. It is a first-in-class oral RAS(ON) inhibitor that blocks the active form of mutant and wild-type RAS, the pathway that drives more than 90% of these cancers. In the phase 3 RASolute 302 trial, median overall survival was 13.2 months with daraxonrasib and 6.7 months with chemotherapy. An accompanying editorial in the New England Journal of Medicine, “Breaking the RAS Barrier in Pancreatic Cancer” (Sivakumar, July 23, 2026), discusses what this means for patients.

    About the lab

    Our approach

    We follow each patient's tumor from the operating room into the laboratory and back to the clinic. Tissue removed at surgery is tested against drugs, profiled in molecular detail and linked to the patient's clinical course, so that what we learn can inform treatment and the design of clinical trials.

    1. Tissue acquisitionTumor tissue is collected from core needle biopsies and during robotic and other minimally invasive operations, together with the patient's clinical data.
    2. Ex vivo drug sensitivity modelsThin slices and core cultures of the living tumor are treated with candidate drugs to measure how that tumor responds.
    3. Multiomic profilingPatient tissue and the matched lab models are studied by DNA and RNA sequencing, pathology and spatial methods that map tumor and immune cells in place.
    4. AI integrationWe use machine learning, foundation models and large language models to synthesize the multiomic data with real-world clinical data and predict treatment response and resistance patterns.

    Research

    Current projects

    Each project starts with tissue from patients treated in our program.

    Organotypic slice culture of core biopsies

    We cut standard-of-care core needle biopsies and surgical specimens into thin slices that keep the tumor and its surrounding cells intact. The slices are treated with the chemotherapy and targeted agents a patient might receive, and response is measured within days by a viability assay and by histology for tumor density, proliferation and cell death. The goal is a practical, real-time sensitivity test that can inform treatment of metastatic pancreatic cancer.

    Manuscript under review at Clinical Cancer Research

    RAS inhibitor response in patient tissue

    We contributed to work led by the McFall laboratory, using slice cultures of treatment-naive biopsies to test the RAS(ON) inhibitor daraxonrasib in tumors carrying different KRAS mutations. Slices from a KRAS G12R tumor lost more viability and nuclear density than slices from a KRAS G12D tumor, and immunostaining for phosphorylated ERK showed pathway suppression in the G12R tissue that was not seen in the G12D tissue at 48 hours. The study connects allele-specific RAS biology to the choice of salvage therapy after resistance.

    Dorbin, Herrera, Davidson et al. Cancer Research 2026, Figure 1N–O

    Drivers of neoadjuvant response and outcome

    The LaBahn Pancreatic Cancer Program takes a unified approach to neoadjuvant therapy, delivering chemotherapy followed by chemoradiation before surgery. The program has conducted several neoadjuvant clinical trials with novel designs that ask which precision and adaptive approaches best optimize response before resection. All resected tumors undergo comprehensive genomic profiling (CGP), and the resulting genomic and transcriptomic data are linked back to clinical and surgical data to identify drivers of response to neoadjuvant therapy and of outcomes after resection.
